Section 01
Three approaches
Objective of onboarding: User completes registration without drop-off and lands on the home page.
Three hypotheses on how to get a Day 1 user to that point. Skipping the AI-first moment in O1 or O2 routes to O3 — user still completes registration and lands on home. All three end with a guided tour. O1 and O2 include an optional pre-registration query — if it lands well, it can be a hook. That is a bonus, not the objective.

Section 02
Shared mechanic — O1 and O2
Both flows depend on two moments that are the real hypothesis — not the steps before them. If these don't land, the flow design around them is irrelevant.
Second-turn ask
After the first AI response, the assistant makes the registration ask conversationally — anchored to what just happened. Not a form that appears out of nowhere. The transition copy matters as much as the UX.
"That's a good one. I can keep you updated on this — takes 10 seconds. What's your name?"
Hook card on home screen
Post-OTP, the home screen surfaces a card directly tied to the first interaction. Home is a continuation of the conversation — not a generic landing page. The guided tour starts here.
User asked about India match → live score + "Set match alert." User asked about a film → cast + "Follow updates."
Section 03
Cohort plan for FnF
FnF users skew urban, educated, and digitally comfortable — they are not representative of real Bharat users. Assign cohorts deliberately so each flow is stress-tested by the right people.
Flow Who Their job in FnF Primary signal Size
O1 25–40, urban, bilingual. Have used ChatGPT or similar before. Find where open-ended AI response breaks. Try edge cases and unexpected queries. First query distribution + second-turn conversion rate 20–30
O2 30–55, metro + Tier 2 mix. Jio subscribers, not active AI users. Non-English primary language preferred. Validate topic tile resonance. Does the AI response on guided topics land well enough to convert? Topic tile selection spread + D1 return rate 40–50
O3 45+, Jio subscribers, low digital confidence. Finance, legal, ops colleagues. Parents of team members. Complete the funnel cleanly. Flag where steps feel unclear or unnecessary. Step-by-step funnel completion + language picker usage 20–30

Section 05
What we are not testing at FnF
✕
Long-form profile building during onboarding
Family role + 6 interest categories is too many steps before registration. Home screen cannot yet deliver on the personalisation promise those choices imply at FnF.
✕
Language picker as Step 1 in O1 and O2
For a voice-first product, language is better inferred through interaction than declared upfront. Auto-detect from device locale + voice handles the baseline.
✕
Forcing voice over text
Mic is offered, text is always available. No user should feel blocked by not wanting to speak first.
!
Guided tour content and depth — TBD
All three flows end with a guided tour but its content is not yet defined. For O3 especially, the tour must actively demonstrate voice — not just show a mic icon — since users arrive with no prior AI interaction.
Section 06
Recommendation
Primary flow
O2 — Guided voice first
Best balance of a good first impression and realistic AI quality at FnF stage. Controlled topics mean higher confidence responses. Most users at FnF will benefit from a starting point.
Fallback + skip destination
O3 — Traditional
For users who skip the AI-first moment in O1/O2, have existing Jio context, are on low-end devices, or simply prefer to set up on their own terms.
Variant
O1 — Open-ended
Run for the digitally confident FnF cohort. First-query distribution data tells us what JBIQ is perceived as before we define it ourselves. Valuable learning even if conversion is lower.
